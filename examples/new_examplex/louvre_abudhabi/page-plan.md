# Page plan

| Page | Goal | Composition |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | Establish the museum as a nocturnal cultural landmark. | Full-bleed night photograph; large editorial title; subtle editable star geometry. |
| P2 | Make the “rain of light” experiential. | Daylight interior photograph with a restrained paper reading column. |
| P3 | Explain the dome as an environmental instrument. | Dark technical page; editable SVG geometry and official facts. |
| P4 | Reveal the project as a museum-city in the sea. | Panoramic aerial photograph with a narrow editorial header and one data plaque. |
| P5 | Close on the civic intimacy of light and water. | Portrait waterfront photograph, ample paper field, and an editable water trace. |
| P6 | Explain the project’s climate logic. | Light environmental-reading page; editable radial field and verified dimensions. |
| P7 | Show experience as a sequence rather than an object. | Dark cinematic triptych with three spatial conditions. |
| P8 | Explain the dome as a material filter. | Photographic banner and a low, editorial specification band. |
| P9 | State the project’s architectural position. | Deep-blue typographic manifesto with a subdued editable star field. |
| P10 | End with the museum as a public landscape. | Dark coda: framed nocturnal image, final statement, and native light trace. |

The page sequence intentionally alternates night/day, photographic immersion,
technical reading, panorama, and editorial calm. No two adjacent content pages
share the same layout.
