# Page plan — Vertical City Retrofit

1. **Cover / The Vertical City Retrofit** — one atmospheric tower image,
   oversized title, restrained metadata.
2. **The visible problem** — full-bleed aging-tower image with a precise
   annotation layer: heat, isolation, vacancy, and maintenance pressure.
3. **One tower, five systems** — architectural section / exploded axonometric
   showing envelope, energy, circulation, shared space, and care infrastructure.
4. **The retrofit stack** — vertical building section with intervention bands;
   labels sit on the drawing, not in floating cards.
5. **Energy is the first constraint** — native line chart and waterfall showing
   demand, envelope loss, and retrofit sequence; scenario values labeled.
6. **The ground floor is the social switch** — before/after plan diagram that
   converts underused lobbies and parking edges into shared civic space.
7. **A day in the vertical city** — horizontal time-based storyboard from
   morning mobility to evening care, using small pictograms and spatial cues.
8. **Three retrofit archetypes** — comparative architectural diagrams for light,
   deep, and district-linked intervention; no card grid.
9. **What can happen while people stay** — construction phasing timeline with
   decanting logic, resident impact, and reversible work packages.
10. **The economics of sequence** — native waterfall / sensitivity chart showing
    why order of operations changes payback; clearly marked illustrative model.
11. **Decision matrix** — compare options by carbon, disruption, capital,
    resilience, and social value; emphasize trade-offs rather than scores only.
12. **The operating model** — ownership, energy, maintenance, and community
    actors connected in an editable governance diagram.
13. **A 36-month delivery path** — three horizons with measurable gates,
    dependencies, and evidence required at each gate.
14. **Closing / Upgrade in place** — concise thesis, three decisions, and a
    final architectural image or section detail.

## Layout rules

- Use four distinct visual modes: image-led editorial, architectural drawing,
  data-led analysis, and decision framework.
- Use a warm mineral paper background with deep graphite, brick red, oxidized
  green, and one electric blue systems accent.
- Keep diagrams editable and source notes visible; avoid decorative UI cards.
- Typography should be presentation-scale: title 26–34 pt, body 13–17 pt,
  source notes 8–9 pt.
