# Domain paradigms

Detect the domain before choosing layout, typography, color semantics, and
anti-patterns. If the domain is ambiguous and the choice changes the design,
ask the user.

## Business and product

Use a clear argument arc such as context → problem → insight → solution →
evidence → action. KPI cards, comparison bars, diagrams, and selective hero
imagery are appropriate when they clarify the decision.

## Scientific research

Use paper-like structure: background, methods, results, discussion, references.
Each data page should have one main figure and a caption. Cite claims. Use
semantic colors for experimental meaning. Avoid marketing hero pages, feature
cards, and decorative effects.

## Academic thesis

Use chapter flow and formal cover information: institution, title, author,
advisor, and date. Preserve citations and include a references page. Keep
animation minimal and body text readable at defense distance.

## Engineering and technical

Use architecture, flow, sequence, API, specification, benchmark, and code
views. Distinguish system layers and data paths. Do not replace technical
relationships with generic feature cards.

## Medical and clinical

Prioritize evidence, disclaimers, patient safety, and legibility. Label evidence
levels where appropriate. Avoid decorative visuals that compete with clinical
meaning.

## Government and public sector

Prefer accessible typography, restrained color, transparent sources, policy or
program logic, budget views, and implementation timelines. Avoid hype language.
